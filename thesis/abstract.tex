\begin{centering}
\textbf{ABSTRACT}\\
\vspace{\baselineskip}
\end{centering}

Superconducting qubits are a promising platform for realizing fault-tolerant quantum computation. Achieving fault tolerance requires both high-fidelity operations and long coherence times. This thesis addresses these challenges through efficient gradient-based pulse optimization for the former and a dynamical coherence-engineering protocol for the latter. On the control side, this thesis revisits GRAPE with hard-coded gradients, circumventing the memory bottleneck of automatic differentiation. Systematic scaling analyses and benchmarks of different gradient strategies further yield a practical decision tree for selecting the most efficient numerical approach for a given problem. On the decoherence side, this thesis shows that cavity dephasing induced by frequency fluctuations of a nonlinear mode can be suppressed dynamically. A weak off-resonant drive applied to the nonlinear mode creates a flux sweet spot for the cavity frequency. For a cavity coupled to a flux-tunable transmon with representative parameters, Monte Carlo simulations show a \(\sim 20\times\) improvement in the cavity dephasing time. Together, these results provide practical tools for reducing both gate infidelity and decoherence in superconducting circuits, bringing them closer to fault-tolerant operation.
