\chapter{Conclusion and Outlook}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis addressed two obstacles to fault-tolerant quantum
computing with superconducting circuits: the computational cost
of quantum optimal control in large Hilbert spaces, and cavity dephasing caused by flux noise. The
two parts of the thesis developed complementary solutions to
these problems.

Quantum optimal control provides a systematic way to design
microwave pulses that implement target operations while
respecting constraints such as bandwidth, leakage, and
crosstalk. Modern approaches often rely on automatic
differentiation (AD) for gradient evaluation, but in large
Hilbert spaces AD becomes memory intensive. This thesis revisited gradient computation in the GRAPE
framework and derived analytical gradients for key cost contributions. Benchmarks against
full-AD and semi-AD approaches clarified the runtime-memory
tradeoffs and led to a practical decision tree for choosing the
best numerical strategy.


Bosonic cavity encodings are attractive because of the photon-loss bias. That
advantage is weakened when the cavity is coupled to a
flux-tunable nonlinear element, where flux noise causes cavity dephasing. This thesis developed a scheme to improve cavity dephasing: a weak off-resonant drive on the transmon that creates
a dynamical sweet spot and makes the cavity frequency
first-order insensitive to flux fluctuations. Numerical simulations show
an approximately twentyfold enhancement of cavity
dephasing time in a representative device. We apply the scheme to binomial and dual-rail encodings, highlighting the scheme's ability to suppress dephasing simultaneously across multiple pairs of Fock states and across multiple modes.

\section*{Outlook}

We turn next to an outlook on what the author believes are possible interesting directions for
future research building on the results of this thesis. 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Quantum optimal control toward larger Hilbert spaces.}
    Basis-reduction techniques such as matrix product states~\cite{schollwoeck2011mps} and the time-dependent variational principle~\cite{kramer1981geometrytdvp} are receiving increasing attention for large-scale simulations of superconducting-qubit systems. Integrating these methods with GRAPE and hard-coded gradients may improve gate fidelity in large-scale processors.

    \item \textbf{Noise-biased gate operation with stark-induced sweet spot.}
    The stark-induced sweet spot was shown here to improve cavity dephasing and preserve its photon-loss bias during idling. An important next step is to determine whether the sweet-spot drive is compatible with gate-operation drives so as to preserve photon-loss bias during gate operations. More broadly, this setting motivates the study of Floquet theory with multiple drives at different frequencies and the development of efficient simulation methods.
\end{enumerate}
